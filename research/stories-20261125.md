## HEADLINES (3 big)

1. TANKERS HIT MINES IN HORMUZ — Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy says two
   oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire in the Strait of
   Hormuz early on Wednesday while using what Tehran called an "unauthorised
   route"; the vessels were not named and no casualties were reported. Iran
   warned ships to coordinate passage or face greater risk, and told
   Washington it would not restore normal shipping until US demands are met,
   while offering via Qatari mediators to reopen the waterway within 48
   hours if conditions are met. Brent crude traded around $91-92 a barrel.
   Sources: Reuters, Deccan Herald.
2. HOUTHIS STRIKE RAS TANURA REFINERY — Yemen's Houthis say they attacked
   Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, the kingdom's largest at 550,000
   barrels a day and next to a major crude export terminal, causing fires,
   and also attacked Riyadh's financial district in three phases. The
   Saudi-led coalition said air defences intercepted and destroyed four
   drones over Ras Tanura but did not confirm the refinery was hit; NASA
   satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed thick black smoke over an
   area of about 600 square kilometres around the refinery.
   Sources: Reuters, Al-Monitor.
3. SHENZHOU-24 PUTS FIRST PAKISTANI IN ORBIT — China launches Shenzhou-24
   from Jiuquan at 08:18 Beijing time on a Long March-2F rocket; it docks
   with the Tiangong space station at 11:56 after a 3.5-hour flight, and the
   crew enters the station the same afternoon. Aboard are commander Tang
   Hongbo, Wu Yi and Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, 40,
   the first Pakistani to reach orbit and the first foreigner ever to visit
   Tiangong; Ali will spend about a week doing joint experiments and return
   on Shenzhou-23, while Tang and Wu stay about six months.
   Sources: Reuters, Xinhua / China Manned Space Agency.
